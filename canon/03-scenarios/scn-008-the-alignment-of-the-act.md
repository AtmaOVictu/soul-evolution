## The image

Two souls do the same thing. One is aligned with the moment; the other works
against it. The same act — fighting, refusing, grinding — is **good** for one,
**better** for the one who harmonizes both elements, and a **detriment** for
the one out of step. Alignment is not morality; it is a tuning.

## The mechanic

**Alignment is a modifier, not a polarity of good and evil.** The player's
chosen alignment (White/Fire or Black/Air) determines how an act lands:

- an act that **matches** the moment → **good**,
- an act that **harmonizes both** elements → **better**,
- an act **against** the moment → **detriment**.

The same action changes value by alignment and circumstance. This is what makes
"endless advance" (fighting on) a real choice rather than a free pass: whether
advancing is good, better, or a detriment depends on alignment and the state
of the cycle.

## The scene it feeds

A scene where the player's alignment makes a choice land differently than it
would for the other side. The narrator names the act's value without judgment:
*"this act is good for you. it would be a detriment for the other."*

## Canon claim

Instantiates the alignment doctrine (`00-mythos.md`): alignment is a modifier
(good/better/detriment), not morality. This addresses Claude's flagged gap —
"refusal/endless advance" is no longer free; its value is alignment-dependent.
