## Summary (what the document is)

A short song-myth and dialogue. The myth of Dawn (Zarja) — a being who leaves
flesh for archetype, and of Luke who cuts himself free.

## The myth

> "Luke remained alone, but found freedom in solitude.
> **Dawn left from flesh into archetype, from breath into myth.**
> Luke took her knife of the face and cut himself, until pain set him free.
> In the body, a wound; in the spirit, breath; in the soul, freedom."

## The dialogue

> **Luke:** "You are the knife that cut me. When you disappeared as a person,
> you became a myth that remains."
> **Dawn (Zarja):** "I am not flesh. I am cut and shadow."
> **Luke:** "That is why I left you behind — as an archetype I carry in depth."

## Key connections

- **The cut is what frees.** "Luke took her knife and cut himself, until pain
  set him free." This is the wound→freedom doctrine (Corpus Trinitas, Double
  Book) as a living myth — the cut (¬/⊘) is not destruction but liberation.
- **Flesh → archetype.** "Dawn left from flesh into archetype, from breath into
  myth" — a being becomes more by leaving the flesh behind. This is the soul's
  apotheosis — the becoming-a-myth.
- **"In the body, a wound; in the spirit, breath; in the soul, freedom."** — the
  three registers in one line: body (⊥)=wound, spirit=breath, soul=freedom.
- **The knife is the bridge.** The one who cut (Dawn/Zarja) is not the enemy but
  the one who frees — "you are the knife that cut me... as an archetype I carry
  in depth." The cut creates the bridge carried within.
